About PrecisionCardio™
What is PrecisionCardio?
PrecisionCardio (PrecisionCardio Industry Alliance, Inc.) is a new, independent, member-governed nonprofit industry association for cardiovascular therapeutics companies. Members determine which shared precompetitive challenges warrant collective action and direct approved initiatives with appropriate independent partners.
Why does PrecisionCardio need to exist?
Companies developing different cardiovascular therapies often face similar barriers in regulatory science, clinical evidence, care delivery, and demonstrating long-term value. Some are too broad for one program to solve alone. PrecisionCardio creates a standing, member-governed institution for addressing the shared challenges companies decide to take on together.
Is PrecisionCardio focused only on genetics or rare disease?
No. Its scope spans cardiovascular therapeutics across disease areas, modalities, and development stages, from rare inherited conditions to common cardiovascular diseases. Genetic and precision medicine opportunities can be part of the work, but they do not define the association’s full mission.
How is PrecisionCardio different from a professional society or patient organization?
PrecisionCardio is governed by cardiovascular therapeutics companies. Professional societies, patient organizations, and other independent groups have distinct missions and expertise. Where appropriate, they could collaborate on member-selected initiatives; PrecisionCardio does not replace or speak for them.
What is PrecisionCardio’s legal status?
PrecisionCardio Industry Alliance, Inc. is incorporated in Delaware as a nonprofit industry association and is seeking recognition from the IRS as a tax-exempt business league under Section 501(c)(6). Recognition has not yet been granted.
The work
What kinds of work will members do together?
Potential work spans regulatory science and standards, clinical research and evidence, implementation and care delivery, and health economics and value. Examples include collaborative approaches to surrogate endpoint evidence, AI-assisted event-adjudication validation, 90-day HFrEF treatment optimization measures, and durable-value standards for cardiovascular gene therapies. These are illustrations, not approved initiatives. See the four examples.
How are priorities chosen?
Member companies identify shared challenges and decide which warrant collective action. Members elect the Board and vote on priorities in accordance with the association’s governing documents. The founding team does not prescribe a fixed project agenda.
Will PrecisionCardio engage with regulators?
Potentially. A member-approved initiative may benefit from scientific dialogue or input from FDA, CMS, or another agency. Any engagement would occur through established channels, with appropriate independence and safeguards. No regulatory participation, endorsement, or outcome is assured.
How are external partners selected?
The expertise follows the member-selected question. Depending on the initiative, collaborators may include biomarker scientists, clinical events committees, professional societies, health systems, quality-measure experts, health economists, or patient organizations. No external partnerships are confirmed solely by being referenced as examples.
How is the work funded?
Member dues sustain the scientific, strategic, legal, and operational capacity behind member-selected priorities. Funding arrangements for individual initiatives, where additional resources are needed, would be developed and approved through the association’s governance process. Membership is not sponsorship of a predetermined project portfolio.
What does “precompetitive” mean?
It means collaborating on legitimate shared scientific, clinical, methodological, or system-level challenges without coordinating how individual companies compete. The association’s work will operate under appropriate independence, confidentiality, and antitrust safeguards.
What is outside PrecisionCardio’s scope?
Product-specific development choices, confidential trial data, proprietary business strategies, and coordination of product-specific pricing, contracting, or coverage decisions remain outside collective work. PrecisionCardio is not a consultancy conducting proprietary projects for individual members. Specific projects will be reviewed under applicable antitrust and confidentiality safeguards.
Membership and governance
Who can become a member?
Companies developing cardiovascular therapeutics, across disease areas, modalities, sizes, and stages of development. Independent scientific, clinical, regulatory, and health-system organizations may participate as collaborators on relevant initiatives rather than as member companies.
What do founding members receive?
Founding members help shape the association’s governance and initial priorities, take part in electing the Board, and can participate in directing approved collective initiatives. Founding recognition and eligibility for initial leadership roles remain subject to final membership terms and governing documents.
When does founding membership close?
The founding membership period is open through December 31, 2026. Companies interested in participating can begin a membership conversation through the website.
Who governs PrecisionCardio?
PrecisionCardio is governed by its member companies through an elected Board of Directors. Members select the priorities and direct collective initiatives under the association’s governing documents. The founding organizers are establishing the infrastructure; they are not a permanent substitute for member governance.
Does founding membership guarantee a Board seat?
No. Member companies elect the Board under the association’s governing documents. Founding members have an early opportunity to help shape governance and may be eligible for leadership roles, but a Board seat is not automatic.
Have the example initiatives or partnerships already been approved?
No. The four examples show what collective work could look like; they are not approved projects, announced partnerships, or commitments from FDA, CMS, or any other organization. Founding members will help determine which priorities move forward.
