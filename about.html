About

Some questions are bigger than any one program.

Cardiovascular therapeutics companies face many of the same scientific, regulatory and care-delivery challenges, from discovery through postapproval. Most work through them alone, one program at a time.

PrecisionCardio™ was founded to give those companies a dedicated, member-governed home for the challenges they share.

Founded by Megan Sutton, Alla Zarifyan, Leila Berkeley and Daisy Li-Brum. Meet the team.

01  Why we exist

Our mission

Help more patients reach a precise diagnosis and disease-specific care, sooner.

Clinical care informs innovation, and innovation advances care. What companies learn in development shapes how patients are recognized and treated. What happens in care shapes what the next trial needs: the right patients, credible endpoints and comparable data.

Many of the hardest problems along that cycle are at the disease level, upstream of product-specific decisions. No single company owns them, and few programs last long enough to solve them. PrecisionCardio gives member companies a lasting structure to decide which shared problems warrant collective attention, and to carry that work forward.

02  The shared challenges

Different portfolios. The same open questions.

Five challenges that recur across companies, from development through patient care, and what members could do about each one together.

01

Surrogate endpoint validation

When does a change in a measure predict clinical benefit?

A measure can track prognosis without being a valid surrogate. Programs measuring left ventricular mass in Danon disease, Friedreich ataxia cardiomyopathy and HCM face overlapping questions, though each disease needs distinct evidence.

What members could do together

Establish what links treatment-induced change to patient-relevant outcomes in each disease, define the missing evidence, and decide whether to seek nonbinding scientific dialogue with FDA.

02

Clinical trial readiness

Why does every sponsor repeat site qualification?

Outcome trials and advanced-therapy studies rely on many of the same cardiac centers, which answer overlapping surveys and repeat startup steps for each sponsor.

What members could do together

With willing centers, map where startup time goes and build site-maintained capability profiles any sponsor can use.

03

Natural history and external controls

Can existing data support credible comparisons?

Rare-disease programs need natural history and external controls; common-disease programs need event rates. Existing registries were often built for other purposes and measure outcomes differently.

What members could do together

Identify where existing registries overlap, whether they measure the same outcomes, and what data would make them useful across programs.

04

Evidence for coverage and long-term value

What evidence is needed beyond pivotal trials?

Gene therapies, rare-disease treatments and chronic-disease therapies all face questions of durability and long-term outcomes that short trials can’t answer.

What members could do together

Ask independent payer and HTA methodologists which long-term, patient-relevant outcomes make evidence decision-useful.

05

Evidence-based cardiovascular care

Why isn’t proven care reaching patients?

Only 18% of US patients with HFrEF were prescribed all four foundational therapies,1 and unexplained hypertrophy often stops at the echo, before anyone asks what caused it.

What members could do together

Partner with professional societies and health systems to map where evaluation and treatment stall, and what gets them completed.

Examples, not announced projects. Members choose the agenda; no company or collaborator has committed to these.

03  Working with partners

The partner follows the priority, not the other way around.

Most of this work needs expertise and reach beyond the member companies. Members first decide the priority. PrecisionCardio then brings in the partners best placed to help advance it.

Chosen firstA member-selected priority
  • Professional societies
  • Academic groups and clinical experts
  • Health systems
  • Patient organizations
  • Other organizations with relevant capabilities

Partners join where a project needs them. None is a second class of membership. Regulators engage through their established channels.

04  The boundary

At the disease level, the challenge is shared.

Product-specific decisions are not.

Shared at PrecisionCardio
  • Disease-level scientific and clinical questions
  • Endpoints, natural history and trial readiness
  • Evidence on where care stalls
  • Independently developed education and resources
Stays with each company
  • Product-specific development decisions
  • Confidential trial data
  • Proprietary strategies
  • Pricing and contracting
  • Anything competitively sensitive

Collective work runs under an antitrust protocol being developed with counsel.

06  What we are

An independent, member-governed nonprofit industry association.

For cardiovascular therapeutics companies across development and care. Member companies set the agenda, elect leadership and decide where collective action is warranted. PrecisionCardio provides the lasting structure to pursue those priorities and to engage outside partners when needed.
